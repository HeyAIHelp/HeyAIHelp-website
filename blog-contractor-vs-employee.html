Do you need another employee, or do you just need the work done?

By Denise, founder of Hey AI, Help! · October 10, 2026

Most small business owners hit the same wall sooner or later. The paperwork is stacking up, your Google Business Profile has not been touched since spring, and bids are going out a day later than they should. The first thought is almost always the same: I need to hire someone.

Sometimes that is exactly right. But a lot of the time, what you actually need is the work done, not another person on payroll. Those are two very different things, and they come with very different price tags.

An employee costs more than the wage

When you hire a W2 employee, the hourly wage is only the start. According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, benefits make up about 30 percent of what private employers spend on compensation. Put another way, for every dollar you pay in wages, you are spending roughly another 43 cents on top of it.

And that is before the costs that never show up on a paycheck:

  • Payroll taxes and filings. Your share of Social Security and Medicare, plus the quarterly reports that go with them.
  • Insurance. Workers' compensation and unemployment insurance.
  • Time off. Sick time, which Oregon requires you to offer, and paid vacation if you want to keep good people.
  • A place to work. A desk, a computer, software licenses and a phone line.
  • Hiring and training. Writing the job post, interviewing, onboarding, and the weeks it takes before a new person is really up to speed.
  • Your time. Scheduling, supervising, answering questions and doing reviews.

With a contractor, you pay for the work

An independent contractor runs their own business. You are not paying for hours spent sitting at a desk waiting for the next task. You are paying for finished work. There is no payroll to run, no benefits to fund, no workers' comp on your books and no extra desk to set up. You know what it costs each month before the month starts.

When your busy season ends, you do not have to make the hard call about cutting someone's hours. You simply adjust the work.

More experience than you could hire for a few hours a week

Here is the part owners often overlook. A small business usually cannot justify hiring someone with decades of management experience to handle a few hours of office work each week. The budget is just not there for that, and honestly, that person probably would not take the job.

A contractor changes that math. You get the experience for only the hours you need. In my case, that is 20 years in Oregon state government, including assisting the BOLI commissioner with setting prevailing wage rates, followed by running a medical clinic. I am also Claude certified through Anthropic. I mention it not to brag, but because that background is what lets me walk into an office, see where the time is going, and get things running smoothly without a long learning curve.

Faster turnaround

A new employee has to learn how you like things done, where the files live and what a good bid looks like for your business. A contractor who has done this work for other businesses already has the systems, templates and habits in place.

I also do the work with AI, and that makes a real difference in speed. Work that might take a traditional assistant 10 hours, I can usually deliver in about 5. Every piece still gets my own review before it reaches you. The speed comes from the tools. The quality check comes from me.

The benefits nobody thinks about until they need them

  • No hiring gamble. A bad hire can cost you months of time and money before you know it was a mistake. If a contractor is not the right fit, you part ways without the paperwork.
  • The work does not stop for vacations or sick days. Your monthly work still gets done when life happens.
  • Knowledge stays with your business. When an employee leaves, what they knew often walks out the door with them. I write process documents for the tasks your team does repeatedly, so that knowledge stays with you no matter who comes and goes.
  • Easy to scale. Need more help during bid season and less in the winter? Adjust month to month.
  • Fresh eyes. Someone who works with many small businesses sees what works elsewhere and brings it to yours.
  • Your time back. No managing, no scheduling, no performance reviews. You get the finished work and get back to running your business.

When an employee is the right call

I would not be giving you the whole picture if I stopped there. If you need someone answering phones all day, greeting customers at the counter, working on a job site, or doing work you need to direct hour by hour, hire an employee. That is what employees are for.

A contractor is not a replacement for your team. The best use of one is taking the paperwork and the online upkeep off your team's plate, so the people you already have can do the work you hired them for.

A word of caution on classification

Hiring an outside contractor is not the same as calling your employee a contractor, and the state of Oregon takes the difference seriously. A true independent contractor runs their own business, has other clients, uses their own tools and decides how the work gets done. If you are unsure where someone falls, check with your accountant or attorney before you make the change. The penalties for getting it wrong are not small.
